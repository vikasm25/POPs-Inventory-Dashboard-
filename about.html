ABOUT THE PROJECT

Understanding persistent pollutants

An accessible, research-oriented view of where emissions are reported, how they have changed, and which activities contribute.

WHAT IS POPs ATLAS?

Explore global emissions through space, time and source

POPs Atlas is an interactive visualization dashboard for country-level persistent organic pollutant emissions inventories. It combines maps, timelines, source-category summaries and geographic comparisons to make complex published data easier to examine. It is a visualization and analysis tool—not a new emissions inventory or an exposure-risk model.

What are POPs?

Persistent organic pollutants (POPs) are carbon-based chemicals that resist degradation, may accumulate in organisms and food webs, and can be transported over long distances. Some are intentionally manufactured, while others arise unintentionally from industrial and combustion processes.

Why do they matter?

Depending on the substance and exposure, POPs can pose risks to human health, wildlife and ecosystems. Their persistence means contamination can remain relevant long after emissions occur, including far from the original source.

What can this dashboard reveal?

Compare reported emissions across countries and years, identify major contributing source categories, observe broad temporal changes, and investigate the distribution of reported emissions across continents. These are emissions patterns—not direct measures of ambient concentrations or health effects.

Why use inventories carefully?

Different inventories may have different methodologies, sector definitions, geographic coverage and pollutant definitions. Missing observations must not be interpreted as zero, and emissions estimates should not be treated as direct measurements. Report the inventory, pollutant, year and units alongside results.

SCIENTIFIC CONTEXT

From emissions to environmental impacts

Emissions are the starting point of a larger environmental pathway. Atmospheric transport, deposition, partitioning between air, water, soils and sediments, degradation and food-web uptake determine where POPs ultimately accumulate. An increase in emissions does not necessarily produce a proportional change in concentrations everywhere.

The dashboard currently presents published EDGAR v8.1 unintentional POPs emissions, including aggregated PCBs, hexachlorobenzene and selected PAHs. EDGAR's aggregate PCB series is not PCB-28. Toxic-equivalency data for PCDD/F are not combined with chemical mass units.

International action

The Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants is a global treaty aimed at protecting human health and the environment from POPs through measures that include eliminating or restricting certain substances and reducing releases of unintentionally produced POPs.
