About POPs Atlas

A spatial and temporal exploration interface for published persistent organic pollutant emission inventories.

OBJECTIVE & SCOPE

Making emissions inventories interpretable

POPs Atlas connects country-level annual emissions with their geographic distribution, source attribution and change over time.

The platform supports exploratory research, teaching, hypothesis development and communication. It presents published estimates; it does not generate new emissions, simulate atmospheric transport or quantify exposure and risk.

SCIENTIFIC CONTEXT

What the atlas shows

Four ideas help place the dashboard in its proper analytical context.

01 / ENVIRONMENTAL CHEMISTRY

What makes POPs persistent?

Persistent organic pollutants are organic chemicals that exhibit environmental persistence and, for many substances, bioaccumulation potential, toxicity and long-range transport. Their behavior depends on molecular structure, degradation kinetics, temperature-dependent partitioning, environmental media and food-web interactions.

POPs can partition among air, aerosols, water, soil, sediment and biota. Emission location and environmental concentration therefore need not coincide.

02 / EMISSIONS DATA

Inventory definitions and units

Emission inventories estimate the mass released by geographic unit, source category, pollutant and year. POPs Atlas publishes EDGAR v8.1 data for unintentional POPs, including aggregate PCBs, HCB and selected PAH compounds.

The EDGAR PCB aggregate is not PCB-28 or another individual congener. Toxic-equivalency quantities such as PCDD/F TEQ are not interchangeable with mass-based emissions.

03 / ENVIRONMENTAL FATE

Emissions are not concentrations

Atmospheric movement, deposition, air–water exchange, sorption, degradation, sediment burial and biological uptake mediate the transformation of emissions into environmental burdens.

Emission trajectories can support model interpretation, but the dashboard does not calculate fugacity, deposition fluxes, chemical residence times or concentrations.

04 / ANALYTICAL LIMITATIONS

Coverage and comparability

Inventory completeness varies by substance, year, source sector and reporting geography. A missing observation is not equivalent to a measured or modeled zero, and national estimates may not be directly comparable across independent inventories.

Totals and rankings summarize available observations under the active filters. Interpret them with the inventory's coverage, methods and sector definitions.

CONTEXT & TRANSPARENCY

Policy and provenance

A

The Stockholm Convention

The Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants protects human health and the environment by eliminating or restricting listed chemicals and reducing releases of unintentionally produced POPs.
