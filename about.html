SCIENTIFIC OVERVIEW / EMISSIONS INVENTORIES

About POPs Atlas

A spatial and temporal exploration interface for published persistent organic pollutant emission inventories.

OBJECTIVE & SCOPE

Making emissions inventories interpretable

POPs Atlas provides an interactive interface for investigating the magnitude, geographic distribution, source attribution and temporal evolution of reported emissions. Its primary outputs are country-level annual emissions, expressed in tonnes per year (t/year), with sectoral and regional summaries derived from the selected inventory.

The platform is intended for exploratory research, teaching, hypothesis development and communication. It does not generate new emissions estimates, perform atmospheric transport simulations or quantify exposure and risk.

01 / ENVIRONMENTAL CHEMISTRY

What makes POPs persistent?

Persistent organic pollutants are organic chemicals that exhibit environmental persistence and, for many substances, bioaccumulation potential, toxicity and long-range transport. Their behavior depends on molecular structure, degradation kinetics, temperature-dependent partitioning, environmental media and food-web interactions.

POPs can partition between the gas phase, aerosols, surface waters, soils, sediments and biota. Consequently, emission location and environmental concentration need not coincide.

02 / EMISSIONS DATA

Inventory definitions and units

Emission inventories estimate the mass released by geographic unit, source category, pollutant and year. POPs Atlas currently publishes EDGAR v8.1 data for unintentional POPs, including aggregate PCBs, HCB and selected PAH compounds.

The EDGAR PCB aggregate must not be interpreted as PCB-28 or any individual congener. Toxic-equivalency quantities such as PCDD/F TEQ are not interchangeable with mass-based emissions.

03 / ENVIRONMENTAL FATE

Emissions are not concentrations

Atmospheric advection, deposition, air–water exchange, sorption, degradation, sediment burial and biological uptake mediate the transformation of emissions into environmental burdens. The resulting concentration response can be delayed, nonlinear and geographically displaced.

Comparing emission trajectories can support model interpretation, but the dashboard does not calculate fugacity, deposition fluxes, chemical residence times or concentrations.

04 / ANALYTICAL LIMITATIONS

Coverage and comparability

Inventory completeness varies by substance, year, source sector and reporting geography. A missing observation is not equivalent to a measured or modeled zero. National estimates are not necessarily directly comparable across independent inventories.

Continental totals and rankings summarize available observations under the selected filters. They should be interpreted alongside methodological uncertainty, geographic coverage and sector definitions.

INTERPRETING THE DASHBOARD

From source attribution to research questions

Use the inventory and pollutant selectors to define a consistent analytical scope. Source-category filters identify contributing activities, while the year slider and time-series plot reveal changes over time. Country rankings indicate the distribution of reported emissions; they are not a measure of population exposure or ecological damage.

International shipping and other non-national reporting categories may contribute to inventory totals but should not be ranked as countries. Territorial representation follows the displayed boundary dataset and does not constitute a statement about sovereignty or recognition.

POLICY CONTEXT

The Stockholm Convention

The Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants is an international treaty designed to protect human health and the environment by eliminating or restricting listed chemicals and reducing releases of unintentionally produced POPs.
